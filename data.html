This is the schema tipstar.features.build_race, tipstar.trainers, and tipstar.audit expect. Nothing in tipstar/ cares where the data came from - your DataSource implementation (see tipstar/datasource.py) is responsible for producing exactly this shape from whatever real feed you point it at. tipstar/mock_datasource.py is a fully worked example that satisfies this contract with synthetic data.

Assumed scope: AUS-style greyhound racing, fields of 4-8 runners.

DataSource.historical_results(date_from, date_to) -&gt; Iterable[RaceCard]

Same RaceCard shape as event(), but only for settled races (i.e. results populated) in the inclusive date range. Used by tipstar.trainers.refresh_trainer_strike_rates and tipstar.audit.

Notes

  • last_twenty_starts characters: digits are finishing position, x/f/t/s

(case-insensitive) are non-finishes (scratched/fell/etc). Anything else is ignored.

  • flucs_with_timestamp.last_six[].timestamp needs to be ISO-8601

parseable; tipstar.features.snapshot_odds uses it to rewind a settled card to how it looked before the jump, for lookahead-free auditing.

  • Prices are decimal odds, not fractional or American.
  • Everything is deliberately vendor-neutral. If your real provider's

field names differ, translate them into this shape inside your own DataSource class rather than changing tipstar/features.py.

Model outputs are relative-value estimates from an unvalidated model — not outcome predictions and not financial or betting advice.
