Two people. One town. One rule you can carry.

Pip reads the small print. Bo lifts what Pip cannot. In Brackwater, a rule is a brass plate you move from one socket to another, and the pair have one morning to open the sea door.

Meet the pair
  • Two players
  • One screen
  • No timer
  • No fail state

Neither of them can open a room alone.

Pip is small, quick, and reads what is written small. Bo is tall, heavy, and carries the half that will not move. Every gate has two halves, and the halves take turns.

Bo can carry.

  • Take hands

    Either player offers. The other accepts. The accepter leads, and the player who offered follows.

  • Offer a boost

    Bo lifts. Pip decides. Nothing happens until she says yes.

  • Call

    One button, one line across the room. In the shed it is the only way back to each other.

  • Keep the clip

    A hair clip on the rope loft. It grants nothing. It comes home in her pocket.

A rule is a thing you can pick up.

Every rule is two brass plates: a subject and a condition. Bo carries the subject. Pip fits the condition. The socket holds one sentence, so a new sentence makes a new room.

The stakes are small. The town is not watching.

Brackwater is a working harbour where a brass sentence is a municipal fact, like parking. Nobody there finds one strange. Nothing can hurt a player: no fall, no fire, no fight.

One morning to open the sea door.

The town asks the two of them to open it. One holds. One fits. The morning takes a piece of what each is proudest of: her care, his strength. Then it takes the sunrise, because they were busy doing the job.

Built for a pair, not a solo player.

The pair this is built for: one person plays games every week, the other almost never. Both should finish a room feeling clever. No puzzle here asks both players to act in the same short window, so nobody has to be fast.

  • Couples

    The tender moves are optional. Nobody has to perform affection to get through a door; the game just leaves the room for it.

  • Friends

    Split one keyboard, or bring two pads. One holds, one climbs, then you swap and the other one learns the room.

  • A parent and a child

    No timer, no fail state, no reading speed test. The puzzle waits for as long as it takes.

  • No death

    Nothing in the game can hurt either player.

  • No timer

    Puzzles wait. The pause menu stops both players.

  • Turns, not reflexes

    Coordination, never simultaneity. One moves, then the other.

  • One screen

    Local only. Two pads, or two halves of one keyboard.

  • Subtitles and reduce motion

    Both sit in the options screen, next to the glyph choice.

  • One pause

    The pause menu stops the whole simulation, so nobody moves while you read.

One scheme serves every seat: bottom does the thing, left takes hands, top calls, right goes back. The pad picture and the key table say the same thing, so a player who does not read button names can still find them.

Full remapping is still to come. Until then the map is fixed and printed in the game, rather than half-built.

Playable now. Not finished.

A five-room slice runs end to end: the quay, the shed, the rope loft, the gatehouse and the sea door. Two endings, a save file, a menu with options. It is not a finished game, and this page will not pretend it is.
