This document is the fundamented reference for the model: what it does, exactly how, and the reasoning behind each decision (including the ones the walk-forward analysis in analysis/README.md forced us to accept). It is the single place an agent or human goes to understand the model before touching it.

1. What the model does

Races AUS greyhound fields (4–8 runners) into win probabilities and compares them to the market's own implied probabilities to find relative value — runners the model thinks are more likely to win than their fixed-odds price implies. It then produces a capped, diversified tip list.

It is a relative-value estimator, not a winner predictor: a high edge means "underpriced relative to the market," not "will win." See §7 for what it is not.

The pipeline (what actually runs, in order)

train()  [tipstar/service.py]
   for each settled race in [date_from, date_to]:
       trainer_sr = refresh_trainer_strike_rates(cards strictly BEFORE the race)
       rows, meta = features.build_race(card, trainer_sr)
       if rows: pairs.append((rows-feats, winner_index))
   coef = model.train(pairs, iters, lr, l2)
   store.kv_set("coef", coef)            # persisted; survives restarts

tips()  [tipstar/service.py]
   coef = store.kv_get("coef") or model.EXAMPLE_COEF   # inert fallback
   for each upcoming card:
       rows, meta = features.build_race(card, trainer_sr)
       model.score(rows, coef)          # -> p_model, edge, rank per runner
       picks = qualify(rows, ...)       # or qualify_all_top for full coverage
       tips  = diversify(picks, ...)[:max_tips]
       overlay(tips)                    # cross-book price check (see GAPS.md)

train and tips are decoupled: training is a one-time + periodic offline pass (the retro/train CLI and the first-run backfill in install.bat); tips just loads the last trained coef and scores. If no coef has been trained yet, tips runs on the inert EXAMPLE_COEF (§5).

2. Scoring: conditional logit (softmax over runners)

model.score(rows, coef) (in tipstar/model.py):

  1. Linear utility per runner: u_i = Σ_j coef[j] · feat_ij.
  2. Softmax across the runners in that race:

p_model_i = exp(u_i − max) / Σ_j exp(u_j − max) (max-subtracted for numeric stability).

  1. Market probability: p_mkt_i is the overround-normalised implied

probability from fixed_win prices — 1/price, then renormalised to sum to 1 across the race (computed in features.build_race).

  1. Edge: edge_i = p_model_i / p_mkt_i. An edge of 1.30 means the model

thinks the runner is 30% more likely to win than its price implies.

  1. Runners are re-sorted by p_model descending and assigned rank (1 = best).

Why conditional logit rather than scoring runners in isolation: a win is a relative event — a runner's chance only has meaning against the field. Softmax guarantees Σ p_model_i = 1 within the race and makes the model learn within-race contrast, which is exactly the structure of the problem. An independent per-runner classifier could (and does, naively) output probabilities that don't sum to 1 and ignores field strength.

Why divide by p_mkt (edge) instead of betting on p_model directly: the whole value proposition is beating the market's number. p_model alone says "how likely" — edge says "am I more confident than the odds." Since the market is the thing we're staking against, the ratio is the actionable signal.

Design decisions, and why:

  • Race-normalised z-scores (bt_z, settle_z, pm_z, age_z) rather than

raw values: a 29.5s best time means different things on a 300m dash than a 600m. Z-scoring within the race makes the feature "this dog is fast for this field," which is what matters.

  • trainer_sr is leakage-free by construction: refresh_trainer_strike_rates

builds the trainer table only from races before the race being scored (tipstar/audit.py does this explicitly for the walk-forward check). Reusing today's table to score last week would leak and flatter the record.

  • build_race refuses fields <4 or >8 runners and drops scratched /

non-primary-market runners: the model was fit on 4–8 fields; a 2-runner field or a 12-runner special has different structure and would corrupt the softmax.

  • snapshot_odds(card, lead_seconds) rewinds a settled card's odds to

start − lead_seconds (2.5h) using flucs_with_timestamp, so audits score past races on the prices that were actually available at decision time — no lookahead. This is the same no-lookahead rule the fluc features use.

4. Fitting (tipstar/model.py::train)

Plain-Python gradient ascent on the multinomial (conditional-logit) log-likelihood — no numpy, deliberately, to keep the whole pipeline dependency-free. For each iteration it accumulates the per-feature gradient observed − expected (the standard GLM score) across all training races and takes a step w += lr·grad/|data| with light L2 shrinkage (l2=1.0 by default, scaled by 1/|data|).

Why L2: several features — the barrier dummies especially — are sparse and easy to overfit on ~a few hundred–thousand races. Mild shrinkage keeps the coefficients sane without killing a real signal. Why it's "good enough": a few thousand races × 20 features trains in seconds. It's not tuned for anything larger; if the store grows to millions of races, swap in a real solver without changing the feature contract or the score/edge/selection logic.

The training pair is (feats, winner_index): winner_index is the position of the runner who actually finished 1st, recovered from results on a settled card. This is the ground truth the log-likelihood maximises.

5. The honest state of the model (read this before believing an edge)

model.EXAMPLE_COEF is a placeholder, not a model. It sets every weight to 0 except lp_mkt = 1.0. The softmax of log(p_mkt) (renormalised) is exactly p_mkt again, so scoring with it makes edge = 1.0 for every runner, always. It exists only so --mock --dry runs produce sane, non-crashing output. Nothing in this repo ships a pre-fitted, pre-validated model.

A real coef only appears after train() runs on at least ~20 settled races. install.bat does a one-time 13-day backfill + train the first time you use it, so a fitted model is usually present by the first nightly run — but until then, tips scores on the inert placeholder.

What the walk-forward analysis actually found (full detail in analysis/README.md, reproducible via its scripts):

  1. The market is roughly efficient. Model full-learn log-loss 1.4839 vs the

market's own implied 1.4784 — the model is marginally worse than the blind market favourite. It is essentially re-estimating the market's odds and is locked to the vig. Coefficients confirm it: lp_mkt and lp_mkt_sq dominate.

  1. Raw edge is anti-calibrated. Top-pick hit rate by model edge: <1.0 → 48%,

1.0–1.3 → 41%, 1.3–1.7 → 29% (worst), >1.7 → 22%. So gating on edge ≥ 1.30 (the current selection) selects exactly where the model is least accurate. The value signal that does show a positive ROI is EV = model_prob × price clearing the vig, not raw edge (EV ≥ 1.15 in the 2–4 price band was the one positive lever: +6.8% on 73 bets — small sample, treat as "not negative," not "profitable").

  1. **The current edge ≥ 1.30 gate is therefore a known-weak choice kept for

stability and simplicity, not a validated optimum. This is the single most important thing to know if you're tempted to "improve the model" — the data says the lever is EV-based selection + decision-time price movement, not a better edge threshold. Do not over-sell an edge; the honest read is a thin, probably-noise edge anchored on price.**

This is why the dashboard and all reports carry the disclaimer that outputs are relative-value estimates from an unvalidated model, not outcome predictions or betting advice.

6. Selection and the Top 6 shortlist

Selection (tipstar/selection.py), applied per race by tips() and the retro backtest (both use Service.DEFAULTS, mirrored in retro.DEFAULTS):

  1. qualify — keep only each race's top-rated runner (rank == 1), and

only if it clears the gate: edge ≥ 1.30 and price ∈ [3.00, 12.00]. Narrow favourites (<3) and long-shots (>12) are excluded on purpose — both ends are where a ~20-feature model is least trustworthy, and the vig is worst on the short end. (qualify_all_top is the full-coverage variant: every race's rank-1 regardless of gate, used when the brief is "a tip for every race.")

  1. diversify — cap exposure to any one trainer/kennel or track

(max_per_trainer = 2, max_per_track = 2), so one noisy signal can't dominate the list. Operates on the list already sorted by descending edge.

  1. cap — at most max_tips = 6 selections total.

DEFAULTS = dict(edge_min=1.30, price_min=3.00, price_max=12.00, max_tips=6, max_per_trainer=2, max_per_track=2, form_days=8).

The Top 6 shortlist on the dashboard (tipstar/dashboard.py, the "Top 6 · overall" / "Top 6 · daily" toggle and the always-on golden ring) is the same gate and diversification caps, applied two ways by dashboard._shortlist_ids(tips, scope):

  • scope="overall" — the six best across all races: one global walk

of all qualifying tips, highest edge first, with the per-trainer / per-track counters shared across the whole list. This set gets the golden box ring in every view (it's the "the" picks).

  • scope="daily" — six per race day: the counters reset each day, so

each day gets its own six. This is exactly the model's live shortlist and is what the retro backtest reports.

Both are computed offline at build time and shipped as top6All / top6 flags in the dashboard's tip payload; the client just filters and highlights. Keeping the shortlist logic in the same module as the gate it mirrors means the dashboard can't drift from the live selection.

7. What this is / isn't

  • Is: a relative-value estimator versus the market, conditioned on whatever

coefficients you trained. Walk-forward, leakage-free by construction (trainer table and odds both restricted to pre-decision data).

  • Isn't: a winner predictor; a validated edge (it is not — §5); or advice.

~32% book overround on fixed-odds racing means flat-staking any price band loses before the model's quality is even a factor.

For the full "what we deliberately didn't build" list (no notification channel wired, no compliance review, the inert-coef caveat, the drift-gate warm-up, the overlay only fires on qualifying tips), see GAPS.md. For what the data shows, see analysis/README.md. For the raw card schema the whole thing is built on, see docs/DATA_CONTRACT.md.

Rules:

  1. Constants live in one place. The gate (edge_min, price band, caps) is

defined in Service.DEFAULTS / retro.DEFAULTS and mirrored into dashboard.TOP6_*. If you change a constant, change it everywhere it's mirrored and bump the numbers in §6 — a doc that says 1.30 while the code says 1.25 is a bug.

  1. Adding or removing a feature requires: updating FEATNAMES, the §3

table, and (if it's a price-touching feature) confirming it obeys the no-lookahead cutoff in §3. A feature not in the table is an undocumented feature.

  1. **Changing the model's decision (the gate, the diversification, the

shortlist scope) is a fundamented change** — add one line to §5 explaining why, citing the analysis that justifies it (or stating plainly that it's a stability choice pending data, like the current edge gate).

  1. After a real model change: retrain (python -m tipstar.cli train /

retro), regenerate the dashboard, python -m tipstar.cli publish, and verify live — per AGENTS.md. The numbers in §5 (log-loss, hit-rate-by-edge, EV table) are only as good as the last analysis/README.md run; re-run the analysis if you change anything that affects scoring.
